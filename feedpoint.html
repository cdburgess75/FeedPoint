04

End-fed antennas

The two configurations that hang from one support and still work the world.

EFHWEnd-fed half wave
49:1 unun · resonant · multi-band without a tuner

A half-wave radiator fed at the voltage end (~2450 Ω), so it's also resonant on its harmonics. Cut for the lowest band and the even multiples come nearly free:

WireFundamentalHarmonics
130 ft · 40 m80 m40 · 20 · 15 · 10
66–67 ft · 20.3 m40 m20 · 15* · 10
33 ft · 10 m20 m10
  • *15 m rides the 3rd harmonic of 40 — usable, often high SWR; a tuner touch-up fixes it.
  • A ~34 µH loading coil at ~66 ft turns the 67-footer into an 80–10 m antenna at reduced 80 m bandwidth.
  • Feed end high as practical; sloper, inverted-L, inverted-V all work. The far end is the high-voltage end — keep it away from people.
  • The coax shield is the counterpoise: give it 0.05 λ before a 1:1 choke, or add a short counterpoise wire at the unun.
  • Start 3–5% long and trim — resonance drops when wire bends or nears ground.
EFRWEnd-fed random wire
9:1 unun · non-resonant · tuner required

One wire, any convenient shape, deliberately not resonant anywhere you operate. The 9:1 brings the swings into tuner range.

  • Use the wire check view — avoid half-wave multiples on your bands. 71 ft is the sweet spot for 40–10; 84 ft eases 80 m.
  • Counterpoise matters more than on an EFHW: ≥0.05 λ of the lowest band (13–17 ft for 20 m up, ~35 ft for 40 m) on the ground.
  • 1:1 choke ~15 ft from the unun so the tuner sees the antenna, not your shack wiring.
  • The stealth/portable champion — throw it in a tree, tune, operate.
